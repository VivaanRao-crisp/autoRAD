4 · Behind the board: carriage, pulleys and cord

The cord makes four vertical runs between the two moving bearings on the carriage (M1, M2) and the two fixed bearings on standoffs (F0, F1). Paying out 1 mm of cord pulls the carriage down ¼ mm. Solid lines are the near lane (z −36) and dashed lines are the far lane (z −52). Hover over a routing step to highlight it.

cord, near lane z −36 cord, far lane z −52 carriage plate bearings 625ZZ Ø16 return band

Cord route (design_spec §5)

    Why it works: each of the 4 runs gets shorter by the same amount as the carriage drops. So 300 mm of payout is 4 × 75 mm. All four runs are vertical and every one pulls the carriage down. The band pulls it back up, which closes the handles and takes the cord back in.

    Why there are two lanes: the moving axle runs along x, so cord goes over each moving bearing from the near lane (z −36) to the far lane (z −52). The fixed bearings have their axles along z, so each one turns the cord sideways (x) within a single lane.

    6 · Motion: angle vs travel

    The included angle between the grips as the cord pays out. Hover over the chart to read values and click to move the rig there. The dashed line is a perfectly linear 50° → 110°. The bench rig stops at 300 mm. The full attachment would hold 110° up to 400 mm, but the bench rig has no overtravel protection, so keep the pull at or under 300 mm.

    7 · Printed parts (5 models, 16 pieces, ~9 h)

    Each part is drawn in its own frame and to scale, with dimensions in mm. All pin holes are the pin size + 0.3 mm. Print in PETG or PLA at 0.28 mm layers, 3 walls, 25–40 % infill. STL files are in exports/stl/ and print orientations are in parts_list.md.

    P1 · Handle (bell crank) × 2

    • One flat 8 mm plate, origin at the pivot bore. The drawing shows the right handle. The left handle is the same print turned over; don't mirror it in the slicer.
    • The joint hole has a 90° countersink Ø11 on the back face, for an M5 × 25 countersunk screw with its head flush.
    • Fillet the inside corners R5. Bed footprint is about 167 × 167 mm, so you need a bed of at least 170 × 170. ~2.7 h each.

    P3 · Carriage × 1

    • 8 mm plate behind the board (z −20…−12) with a post that sticks 32 mm further back (to z −52).
    • The axle hole goes through the post along x, 24 mm behind the plate's back face (z −44). It bridges without support.
    • The part is handed: seen from the front, the post is on the left. Don't mirror it. Print it with the board-side face down. ~1.3 h.
    • Band hook: two Ø4 holes. Use the lower one, at (4, −5.5).

    P2 · Link × 2 (identical)

    • 120.0 mm between hole centres, 12 wide, 6 thick, end bosses R8, holes Ø5.3. Printed flat, ~0.6 h each. Print one first and test-fit an M5 bolt.

    P5 · Flange washer × 6 (+ spares)

    • Disc Ø20 × 1, bore Ø5.3. One goes on each side of each bearing (M1, M2, F1) to keep the cord on the Ø16 bearing.
    • If 625ZZ bearings can't be bought, print plain pulleys Ø16 × 7 with a Ø5.3 bore (optional_plain_pulley.stl). They have more friction, but the demo still works.

    Bought: 625ZZ bearing × 3 (+1)

    • Bore 5, OD 16, width 5. It runs directly as a pulley: the cord rides on the outer ring. M1 and M2 sit on the carriage axle and F1 sits on its standoff. F0 is listed as a bearing in the cord route, but design_spec §5 lists flanges only for M1, M2 and F1.

    8 · Board: 200 × 290 × 12 plywood (front face shown)

    The board frame has its bottom-left corner at (−100, −20). Print the 1:1 template docs/img/board_template_1to1.pdf at 100 % and check its 100 mm bar. The drawing below is the same layout. The bolt heads on the front face must be flat (countersunk or button head, no more than 3.5 mm proud), because link R passes over the F0 and F1 heads.

    Slot 1 and slot 2 overlap. Both are at x = 0. Slot 1 runs y 117.6 → 198.9 and slot 2 runs y 177.6 → 258.9. They overlap by 21.3 mm, so in the board they are one slot, y 117.6 → 258.9, 141.3 mm long, 5.3 wide. The carriage pin and the guide bolt are always 60 mm apart on the carriage, so they never meet. Cut it as one slot. Check the board stays stiff enough beside it.

    11 · Open points and deliberate simplifications

    Open (from the assembly check, design_spec §8a)

    • At full open, the right arm clears the left-handle spacer by only 0.51 mm.
    • The flange washers overlap the post by 0.5 mm per side. Leave out the two plain washers beside the post.
    • F1 and F0 bearings sit 1 mm behind their lane plane, because the 37.5 and 21.5 mm standoffs don't allow for the 1 mm flange. The 3D model shows this.
    • The wall beside the carriage pin hole is only 1.35 mm thick.
    • The plates in the front stack have only 0.3 mm gaps, which is intended. Washers set the real gap.
    • Slots 1 and 2 merge into one 141.3 mm slot (section 8). The spec doesn't mention this.
    • Bearing F0: the cord route treats it as a bearing, but no flanges are listed for it (P5 says M1, M2, F1). Confirm whether F0 is a 625ZZ with flanges or something else.

    Deliberately simplified (design_spec §9, say so in the report)

    • No housing, no guards, not machine-mountable. No spool and no gears: a 4-part reeved cord replaces them.
    • Grips are Ø20 dowels instead of Ø32, and they sit 20.9 mm apart in z.
    • Hand clearance isn't addressed. Nothing protects against overtravel past 300 mm.
    • Bearing pulleys are Ø16 with a 1.5 mm cord. Return is a rubber band (2–5 N in the cord); there is no anchor spring.
